# Soft-core and the endpoint problem

This page explains the endpoint problem of alchemical calculations, what Q's
default soft-core potential does, and why it has no effect in the FEP files
that QligFEP writes. It assumes the terms defined in
[How Q does FEP](fep-in-q.md).

## The endpoint problem

Consider one atom of a ligand that is real in state 1 and a dummy in state 2,
next to an atom of the environment. Its Lennard-Jones contribution to the
mapping potential is

$$
\lambda_1\,V_{\mathrm{LJ}}(r), \qquad
V_{\mathrm{LJ}}(r) = \frac{A_{ij}}{r^{12}} - \frac{B_{ij}}{r^{6}} .
$$

The repulsive term is a power law that diverges as $r \to 0$. Scaling it by
a small weight does not remove the divergence; it only moves the distance at
which the repulsion reaches thermal energy. Setting
$\lambda_1 A_{ij}/r^{12} \approx kT$ gives a wall position proportional to
$\lambda_1^{1/12}$:

Two things follow.

**For any weight above zero the atom still excludes volume.** The sampling
stays stable without any soft-core, which is why Q runs dual-topology FEP
routinely. What suffers is convergence. The thermodynamic-integration
integrand $\langle \partial V/\partial\lambda \rangle = \langle \varepsilon_2 - \varepsilon_1 \rangle$
grows as $\lambda_1^{-3/4}$ when $\lambda_1 \to 0$ for an $r^{-12}$ repulsion.
The singularity is integrable, but it needs closely spaced windows near the
endpoints and is slow to converge.

**At a weight of exactly zero there is no wall at all.** In the window
$(0, 1)$ the atoms of ligand A feel no nonbonded force. Solvent and protein
atoms move through them. `qdyn` still evaluates $\varepsilon_1$ for these
configurations, with ligand A fully real, and writes it to the energy file.
For a pair at 1.2 Å in the example above,

$$
V_{\mathrm{LJ}}(1.2\ \text{Å}) \approx 5 \times 10^{4}\ \text{kcal/mol}.
$$

`qfep` then reconstructs the energy difference to the neighbouring window. With
the default QligFEP schedule the first step is $\Delta\lambda = 0.001$:

$$
V_{m+1} - V_m = \Delta\lambda\,(\varepsilon_1 - \varepsilon_2)
\approx 0.001 \times 5 \times 10^{4} = 50\ \text{kcal/mol} \approx 90\,kT .
$$

A small step does not help, because the state energy is unbounded. Samples
like this contribute nothing to the exponential average, so the estimate for
the endpoint pair rests on the few configurations with no overlap. The
endpoint windows are therefore the least reliable part of the calculation, and
the state energies of the weightless state can be large enough to overflow the
printed columns in the `qdyn` log.

The purpose of a soft-core potential is to remove this divergence.

## Q's soft-core potential

Q's soft-core adds a constant $\alpha$ to $r^6$ in the Lennard-Jones potential
(the 2017 Q manual cites Zacharias, Straatsma and McCammon for soft-core
potentials):

$$
V_{\mathrm{vdW}}(r_{ij}) = \frac{A_{ij}}{(r_{ij}^{6} + \alpha_{ij})^{2}} - \frac{B_{ij}}{r_{ij}^{6} + \alpha_{ij}}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
V_{\mathrm{vdW}}(r_{ij}) = \varepsilon_{ij}\left[\frac{R_{ij}^{*12}}{(r_{ij}^{6} + \alpha_{ij})^{2}} - 2\,\frac{R_{ij}^{*6}}{r_{ij}^{6} + \alpha_{ij}}\right]
$$

for the geometric and arithmetic combination rules. The potential is finite
at $r = 0$ and returns to the normal Lennard-Jones form when $\alpha = 0$. Only
the Lennard-Jones term is changed; the Coulomb term is not. It applies to
interactions that involve at least one Q-atom.

The `[softcore]` section of the FEP file gives one value per Q-atom and per
state. Its meaning depends on `softcore_use_max_potential` in `[FEP]`:

| Setting | Meaning of the `[softcore]` value |
|---|---|
| `off` (default) | $\alpha$ itself, in Å⁶ |
| `on` | the desired potential at $r = 0$, $V_{\max}$, in kcal/mol |

With `on`, `qdyn` solves $V_{\mathrm{vdW}}(0) = V_{\max}$ for each pair. For the
geometric rule this gives

$$
\alpha_{ij} = \frac{-B_{ij} + \sqrt{B_{ij}^{2} + 4\,V_{\max}\,A_{ij}}}{2\,V_{\max}},
$$

and for the arithmetic rule

$$
\alpha_{ij} = R_{ij}^{*6}\;\frac{-\varepsilon_{ij} + \sqrt{\varepsilon_{ij}^{2} + V_{\max}\,\varepsilon_{ij}}}{V_{\max}} .
$$

Two properties of this implementation matter below:

1. $\alpha$ belongs to a **state**. The value given for state $s$ modifies
   $\varepsilon_s$ and nothing else.
2. $\alpha$ is a **constant**. It does not depend on the state weights.

## Why it is dormant in QligFEP calculations

QligFEP writes `softcore_use_max_potential on` and this `[softcore]` section:

```text
[softcore]
! Q-atom   state 1   state 2
1          0         20        ! atom of ligand A
...
40         20        0         ! atom of ligand B
```

Each atom gets a value of 20 kcal/mol in the state where it is a **dummy**,
and 0 in the state where it is **real**. Follow an atom of ligand A:

- **State 1 (real).** The value is 0, so $\alpha = 0$ and $\varepsilon_1$
  contains the ordinary, unsoftened Lennard-Jones potential.
- **State 2 (dummy).** The atom type is `DUM`, so $A_{ij} = B_{ij} = 0$. The
  formula above gives $\alpha_{ij} = 0$. Even if it did not, a potential with
  $A_{ij} = B_{ij} = 0$ is zero whatever $\alpha$ is.

So the soft-core is switched on only where there is nothing to soften, and
switched off where the divergence is. The mapping potential is

$$
\lambda_1 V_{\mathrm{LJ}}(r) + \lambda_2 \cdot 0 ,
$$

exactly as without a `[softcore]` section. The value 20 has no effect on
forces, energies or free energies. Calculations set up this way are protected
only by the scaled wall of the previous section, and they have the endpoint
problem described there.

## Why moving the value to the real state is not enough

The obvious change is to put the soft-core on the state where the atom is
real. With Q's constant $\alpha$ that trades one problem for another:

- $\varepsilon_1$ becomes bounded, so the endpoint samples are well behaved.
- But $\varepsilon_1$ is softened in **every** window, including $(1, 0)$,
  where ligand A is supposed to be the fully interacting, physical ligand. The
  end state is no longer the molecule you want the free energy of. With
  $V_{\max}$ = 20 kcal/mol the example pair of the figure has 0.12 instead of
  0.29 kcal/mol at 3.0 Å.

Removing the divergence without changing the end states needs a soft-core
whose strength depends on the weight: none when the state has weight 1, full
when it has weight 0. This is how the soft-core potentials of Beutler and
co-workers and of other codes are built. For Q's form it would read

$$
\alpha_{ij}(\lambda_s) = \alpha_{ij}\,(1 - \lambda_s)^{p} .
$$

## What a weight-dependent soft-core requires

Making $\alpha$ depend on $\lambda_s$ is compatible with mixing state energies
linearly in the *dynamics*: each state energy is still multiplied by its
weight. It is not compatible with the *bookkeeping* (see
[Dynamics and bookkeeping](fep-in-q.md#dynamics-and-bookkeeping)):

- $\varepsilon_s$ now depends on the window it was evaluated in:
  $\varepsilon_s(x; \lambda^{(m)})$.
- The energy file of window $m$ holds $\varepsilon_s(x; \lambda^{(m)})$. The
  energy of the same configuration under window $m+1$ is
  $\sum_s \lambda^{(m+1)}_s\,\varepsilon_s(x; \lambda^{(m+1)})$, which cannot
  be formed from the stored numbers.
- The linear reconstruction in `qfep` would silently use the wrong
  $\varepsilon_s$ and return a biased free energy.

A weight-dependent soft-core therefore requires `qdyn` to evaluate each stored
configuration with the soft-core of the neighbouring windows as well, and the
analysis to use those cross-window energies instead of the linear
reconstruction. That is a change to what is computed and stored, not only to
the pair potential.

## Summary

- Scaling a Lennard-Jones potential by a state weight keeps a repulsive wall
  for any weight above zero, and removes it at zero.
- Q stores unscaled state energies and reconstructs neighbouring windows
  linearly. At the endpoints the unscaled energy of the weightless state is
  unbounded, and a small λ step does not compensate.
- Q's soft-core is a constant per state. In QligFEP input it is placed on the
  dummy state, where it does nothing.
- A soft-core that fixes the endpoints without altering the end states must
  depend on the weights, and that requires cross-window energy evaluation.
